AI Storefront Generator

Decision doc · SuperProfile PM take-home · Live: sppm-three.vercel.app · Repo: github.com/madmay247/ai-storefront-generator

In one line: a creator writes two or three sentences about themselves and gets a store they can sell from today: a bio, a free lead magnet, a ₹499 discovery call and a premium 1:1 priced for their niche. They review it on one screen with a live phone preview, change or regenerate any field, and publish to a shareable link. The bet is that the shortest path to a first sale is selling the creator's time, which already exists, not a course they haven't made yet.

1. The problem and what I assumed

The brief names three unknowns: what to sell, how to price it, how to write it. Underneath them is a fourth that I think causes most of the stalling: the creator has nothing ready to sell. An AI can write a beautiful listing for a "30-day fat-loss course", but if the course doesn't exist the creator can't publish it, so the store stays empty. I designed around products a creator can deliver the day they publish.

  • Assumed: the creator is on a phone, in a hurry, and will accept a good draft rather than craft one. So the AI fills everything and the creator's job is to review, not to write.
  • Assumed: most new creators have expertise and an audience but no product inventory. 1:1 time and a free download are the only things they can sell on day one.
  • Assumed: trust matters more than cleverness. A store that invents a credential ("certified by…") or a result ("500+ clients") is worse than a plain one, because the creator either deletes it or gets caught out by followers.
  • Persona narrowed: I treat the primary and secondary personas as one flow. Both end up writing a short description (see decision 1), so the only difference is how much they have to say about their audience.

2. The flow

Also considered: publishing straight after generation, with no review. Fastest to a live link, but prices go live unseen and the creator never reads their own store. One review screen costs about a minute and makes it theirs.

4. What I deliberately did not build

  • Instagram scraping: against Instagram's terms and unreliable; covered above.
  • Payments, checkout and booking calendars: the brief says they're optional, and SuperProfile already has them. The buttons lead to the product page, which is where they'd plug in.
  • Accounts: they would slow the first session, which is what this product exists to speed up.
  • Hindi / Hinglish output: high value for this audience, but needs a set of test cases to check quality. First thing in the next two weeks.
  • Courses, ebooks and AI-made files: the creator can't sell what doesn't exist yet. Generating a whole ebook would mean low-quality content under their name.
  • Analytics: useful after there is traffic; it doesn't help a creator get to their first publish.

5. How the AI part works

  • One endpoint, one call: gpt-4o-mini at temperature 0.7, chosen for speed (a draft in a few seconds) and cost. The output is strict JSON schema, so the model can only return the exact fields the page needs: name, link, bio, niche (from a fixed list), the lead magnet, two sessions with durations from allowed values, and the community or nothing.
  • Prompt: the model writes as a direct-response copywriter in the creator's own voice, outcome first. Each field carries its own rules in the schema: bio under 110 characters; descriptions open with the buyer's result, then three "•" lines of exactly what they get, then who it's for. Vague words ("journey", "unlock", "guidance") are banned.
  • Quality control: never invent a name, credential, number, testimonial or result. If no name is given, the name is left blank and the creator fills it in. Prices are set in code, not by the model. Community is checked twice. Publishing is blocked until each live product has its required fields.
  • Failure handling: empty input can't be submitted; long input is capped at 500 characters; a failed or refused model call shows a plain message plus a "Set up manually" button that opens the same review screen with blank products, so there's never a dead end. A failed per-field regenerate keeps the old text and shows an error under it.
  • Known gaps: no timeout or automatic retry on the model call yet, and gibberish input ("asdf") isn't detected before drafting. Both are on the two-week list.

7. Launch thought

Ship it inside onboarding as the default for 10% of new sign-ups, with "Set up myself" one tap away, and grow the share as the publish rate holds. Outside the product: short Reels showing "Instagram bio to store in 60 seconds" in English and Hinglish, and the WhatsApp preview card on every shared store as free distribution ("Made with SuperProfile" on the public page).

8. With two more weeks

  • Hindi and Hinglish output, with a set of 30 test personas across niches, scored by hand before each prompt change.
  • Pricing that uses audience size and experience, with a one-line "why ₹2,999?" under each price.
  • A low-price digital product slot (ebook or template pack, ₹199–999) with an AI-drafted outline the creator fills in, so larger audiences have something scalable.
  • Reliability: model timeout with one retry, gibberish and one-word input detection that asks a follow-up question, and per-creator rate limits.
  • Instagram via the official API for creators with business accounts, feeding bio and recent captions into the prompt.
  • Views and clicks on the public page.

9. How I used AI coding tools

I built this with Claude Code. I decided the flow, the product ladder and the pricing approach myself, then used Claude Code to write and refactor components, tighten the prompt and schema against sample creator descriptions, and review the code for bugs (for example, it found the editor preview and public page had drifted apart, which led to the shared component). Starting point: a dashboard layout modelled on SuperProfile's own creator dashboard, so the flow would feel familiar to its users. No UI kit; icons from lucide-react.
