SuperStore

Decision doc · SuperProfile PM take-home · Live: trysupercreator.vercel.app · Repo: github.com/madmay247/superstore

In one line: a creator writes two or three sentences about themselves and gets a store they can sell from today: a bio, a free lead magnet, a ₹499 discovery call and a premium 1:1 priced for their niche and audience size, each with a one-line reason for the price. They review it on one screen with a live phone preview, change or regenerate any field, and publish to a shareable link. The bet is that the shortest path to a first sale is selling the creator's time, which already exists, not a course they haven't made yet.

1. The problem and what I assumed

The brief names three unknowns: what to sell, how to price it, how to write it. Underneath them is a fourth that I think causes most of the stalling: the creator has nothing ready to sell. An AI can write a beautiful listing for a "30-day fat-loss course", but if the course doesn't exist the creator can't publish it, so the store stays empty. I designed around products a creator can deliver the day they publish.

  • Assumed: the creator is on a phone, in a hurry, and will accept a good draft rather than craft one. So the AI fills everything and the creator's job is to review, not to write.
  • Assumed: most new creators have expertise and an audience but no product inventory. 1:1 time and a free download are the only things they can sell on day one.
  • Assumed: trust matters more than cleverness. A store that invents a credential ("certified by…") or a result ("500+ clients") is worse than a plain one, because the creator either deletes it or gets caught out by followers.
  • Persona narrowed: I treat the primary and secondary personas as one flow. Both end up writing a short description (see decision 1), so the only difference is how much they have to say about their audience.

2. The flow

Also considered: publishing straight after generation, with no review. Fastest to a live link, but prices go live unseen and the creator never reads their own store. One review screen costs about a minute and makes it theirs.

4. What I deliberately did not build

  • Reading Instagram profiles: scraping is against Instagram's terms, and the official way needs Meta's approval; covered above.
  • Payments, checkout and booking calendars: the brief says they're optional, and SuperProfile already has them. The buttons lead to the product page, which is where they'd plug in.
  • Hindi / Hinglish output: high value for this audience, but needs a set of test cases to check quality. First thing in the next two weeks.
  • Courses, ebooks and AI-made files: the creator can't sell what doesn't exist yet. Generating a whole ebook would mean low-quality content under their name.
  • Analytics: useful after there is traffic; it doesn't help a creator get to their first publish.

5. How the AI part works

  • One endpoint, one call: gpt-5.4-mini with low reasoning effort. Of five models run on the same 8 creators, it was fastest among the good writers (~4 s vs ~9 s for gpt-4o-mini) with almost no rule breaks; gpt-5.5 wrote slightly better at ~6 s. The output is strict JSON schema, so the model can only return the exact fields the page needs: name, link, bio, niche (from a fixed list), the lead magnet, two sessions with durations from allowed values, and the community or nothing.
  • Prompt: the model writes as a direct-response copywriter in the creator's own voice, outcome first. Each field carries its own rules in the schema: bio under 110 characters; descriptions open with the buyer's result, then three "•" lines of exactly what they get, then who it's for. Filler words ("journey", "unlock", "tailored", "next level"), exclamation marks and session lengths in the text are banned; the length is its own field, so text never goes stale when the creator edits it.
  • Quality control: never invent a name, credential, number, testimonial or result. If no name is given, the name is left blank for the creator to fill in, but the store link is still made from their topic (e.g. /cfa-prep) and doesn't change when they edit the name. Prices are set in code, not by the model. Community is checked twice. A draft that breaks a copy rule gets one rewrite naming the problem; a final code pass swaps leftover filler for plain words, strips exclamation marks and durations, and normalises bullets. An incomplete product stays offline; it never blocks the store from publishing.
  • Failure handling: empty input can't be submitted; long input is capped at 500 characters; gibberish ("asdf") gets a question back instead of a store; each model call times out after 15 s and is retried once; a failed or refused call shows a calm message plus a "Set up manually" button that opens the same review screen with blank products, so there's never a dead end. A failed regenerate keeps the old text with a short note. If saving the store fails, the dashboard says the changes aren't live yet, with Try again, instead of claiming it's live. Large photos are shrunk before upload.
  • Known gaps: the model still invents plausible deliverable details ("5 eggless cake recipes", "a 6-week plan") that the creator has to check, and there are no per-creator rate limits yet.

7. Launch thought

Ship it inside onboarding as the default for 10% of new sign-ups, with "Set up myself" one tap away, and grow the share as the publish rate holds. Outside the product: short Reels showing "Instagram bio to store in 60 seconds" in English and Hinglish, and the WhatsApp preview card on every shared store as free distribution ("Made with SuperProfile" on the public page).

8. With two more weeks

  • Hindi and Hinglish output: let creators get their store written in Hindi or Hinglish, not just English.
  • Instagram handle as input: get Meta's approval for its Business Discovery API, then read the creator's bio, follower count and recent posts from just their handle. Private or personal accounts fall back to the short description.
  • A low-price digital product (ebook or template pack, ₹199–999), with an AI-written outline the creator fills in, so creators with big audiences have something that sells without their time.
  • Pricing that learns from real bookings: raise or lower suggested prices based on which ones creators actually get booked at.

9. How I used AI coding tools

I built this with Claude Code. I decided the flow, the product ladder and the pricing approach myself, then used Claude Code to write and refactor components, tighten the prompt and schema against sample creator descriptions, and review the code for bugs (for example, it found the editor preview and public page had drifted apart, which led to the shared component). It also ran an 8-creator audit script that flagged rule breaks, which drove the copy clean-up and the model choice. Starting point: a dashboard layout modelled on SuperProfile's own creator dashboard, so the flow would feel familiar to its users. No UI kit; icons from lucide-react.
